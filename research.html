Research

Adversity, caregiving, and development across generations

My research asks how early adversity and trauma shape the people who experience them, and how their effects reach the next generation through caregiving, the parent–child relationship, and the developing brain. I draw on developmental and clinical psychology, neuroscience, and evidence synthesis.

Intergenerational trauma

I study how mothers’ own histories of adversity, including childhood trauma and trauma experienced during pregnancy, relate to how they care for their children and to children’s cognitive and emotional development. Part of this work asks a measurement question: how psychological trauma in pregnancy is defined and assessed across studies and around the world, and what that means for the conclusions we draw.

Parenting in the context of substance use

Families affected by substance use face layered risks. My work examines caregiving behavior and parental reflective functioning, a parent’s capacity to think about their child’s thoughts and feelings, among mothers in substance use treatment, and how these connect to mothers’ early experiences and to their children’s development. I am also developing an observational approach for studying nonverbal mother–infant interaction in this context.

Related work
  • Shin et al. (2025), American Journal of Orthopsychiatry: maternal childhood trauma, caregiving, and child cognitive development
  • Lowell, Shin, et al. (2023): reflective functioning of mothers in substance use treatment (WAIMH World Congress symposium)
  • Shin et al. (in preparation): a new ethogram for nonverbal mother–infant interactions

The maternal brain

Pregnancy and early parenthood are periods of profound change. Using EEG, I have worked on studies of how maternal risk factors, including nicotine dependence and opioid use disorder, relate to mothers’ neural responses, their sensitivity as caregivers, and infant development. I have also contributed to research on whose experiences are represented in maternal brain and perinatal trauma research.

Related work
  • Orchard et al. (2026), Journal of Neuroendocrinology: racial and ethnic diversity in MRI studies of the maternal brain
  • Wall et al. (2025), Journal of Racial and Ethnic Health Disparities: representation in studies of trauma during pregnancy
  • Kelly et al. (2024): racial disparities in maternal brain research (ISDP symposium)

Adversity and emotion regulation in youth

Different kinds of adversity may leave different marks. I study how experiences of threat and deprivation relate to adolescents’ internalizing and externalizing symptoms, and the roles that emotion regulation and identity development play in those links. At Teachers College, I also worked on research on emotion regulation flexibility in civilians and veterans.

Related work
  • Shin et al. (2025), Child Abuse & Neglect: threat, deprivation, and adolescent symptoms
  • Shin et al. (2025): poster at the Association for Psychological Science Annual Convention
